## 1. What the author supplied and decided

- **The idea.** Using alternative data to handle new-title cold start in a recommender,
  and the recommender itself (Anime Compass: ALS retrieval with a LambdaMART reranker,
  built before this project).
- **The research design decisions**, taken by the author after reviewing AI drafts:
  - adopting the strict item-disjoint split, with every title from 2019 on kept out of
    the experimental ALS;
  - M2 vs M1 as the only primary hypothesis; M2 vs M0a kept as a secondary comparison
    and M2 vs M0b added; the instruction that the M0a comparison must not be read as the
    pure contribution of personalization;
  - two fixed popularity baselines (raw and season-normalised);
  - keeping the positive-eligible test users for the primary metric while stating that
    it is a conditional evaluation, and adding an auxiliary population chosen by warm
    history alone;
  - keeping the paired user bootstrap, adding the leave-one-title-out analysis, treating
    the title-resampling bootstrap as descriptive only, and calling the three-tier
    outcome "pre-defined evidence levels";
  - requiring an audit of whether mapped Wikipedia articles existed at prediction time;
  - scikit-learn as a dependency, MAL-only content features, refitting on
    train + validation, running M0b and all sensitivity analyses in the single final
    test, committing locally only, and never committing raw ratings, AniList API data or
    per-user id files;
  - the two approvals: to freeze and train, and later to run the final test once.
- **The rules of conduct**, stated by the author in the briefs: no tuning on the test
  set, no changes to features, learner or protocol to obtain a positive result, no
  description of the simulated experiment as a real-time or online backtest, no
  fabricated or early-reported results.

## 3. Proposals that came from the AI

These were not in the author's briefs. Each was put to the author, who accepted,
changed or rejected it:

1. **Removing cold-train titles and all 2019+ non-candidate titles from the ALS**, which
   was stricter than the brief asked for. Accepted.
2. **A second hypothesis, M2 vs M0a.** Kept by the author as a secondary comparison
   only, with an added M2 vs M0b comparison and an explicit limit on its interpretation.
3. **A pass / partial / fail verdict using a title-resampling bootstrap.** Changed by
   the author: the title bootstrap became descriptive, leave-one-title-out was added,
   and the tiers were renamed evidence levels.
4. **User-disjoint groups** (ranker-train / validation / test by hash). Accepted.
5. **Day 0 as the earliest of three dates, features up to day −2.** Accepted.
6. **Logistic regression as the single learner, with a four-value C grid.** Accepted.
7. **The content-to-ALS ridge mapper and the six content features.** Accepted.
8. **Running M0b and the sensitivity analyses in the same final-test run**, so test
   labels are read once. Accepted.

## 4. Errors the AI made, and how they surfaced

Recorded because they bear on how much the AI's unreviewed statements can be trusted:

1. **First feasibility round, entity mapping.** The pre-specified Wikipedia mapping
   resolved the biggest hits to "season 1" and "(TV series)" articles that did not
   exist before the premiere, so 36 of the 215 mapped top-fifth titles showed zero
   pre-air views. Found by the AI after the pre-specified test had been read; the
   corrected mapping is labelled exploratory there.
2. **First feasibility round, time window.** The AI wrote that the Wikipedia window was
   "strictly pre-air". It ended on UTC day −1, which contains the first hours after an
   after-midnight Japanese premiere. Found on 2026-10-10 during the AniList audit and
   disclosed; this experiment uses day −2.
3. **Course-fit advice.** The AI first inferred, without the syllabus, that the course
   required forecasting an economic indicator. That was wrong and was corrected once the
   author supplied the syllabus.
4. **AniList day −1 rows.** The AI first read the episode marker on day −1 rows as
   evidence that those rows were contaminated. A follow-up check showed the affected
   titles air at exactly 00:00 Japan time and that their day −1 activity is not
   elevated; the report states the corrected conclusion.
5. **Two code defects before the freeze**, both fixed before the freeze commit: a
   manifest comparison that treated a tuple and its JSON list as different, and a
   boundary-check pattern that mistook the catalog's age-rating column for a ratings
   file.

### B.2 Brief asking for the pre-registration

> 我决定采用 FEASIBILITY_REPORT.md 第 8 节提出的严格 item-disjoint split，并开始 Personalized Item Cold-Start 离线实验。

> * Warm ALS：2019 年以前的作品。
