- **Sample.** 10 TV titles that premiered between 2016 and 2020, picked evenly along the MAL member ranking by `anime_coldstart/src/peek_anilist_jikan.py`. It is not a random sample and it has no films, OVAs or ONAs chosen as such.

- **Not done.** No model was trained. Nothing beyond these titles was downloaded. Jikan data is absent: `api.jikan.moe` was unreachable on 2026-10-07 and, according to the project owner, the service has shut down.
- **What the trend data is.** Activity on AniList by AniList users. It is not Wikipedia pageviews and not search volume, and a result on one says nothing about the other.

### 3.6 Leakage risks in the trend rows

- **A UTC date is one day early.** The row for Japan day D is stamped at 15:00 UTC on D-1. On the premiere day itself `trending` was a median 11 times the daily mean of the week before (8 titles with both). Dating rows in UTC puts that spike inside the pre-release window.
- **What a day's row covers is not documented.** The newest row any title had was dated 2026-10-08 in Japan, the day the collection ran, so a row exists before its day is over. Fetched again a median 25 minutes later for 10 titles, `trending`, `popularity` and `inProgress` had changed in 0. So the row is not a live counter, though a slower refresh during the day is not ruled out. The premiere-day jump above says the row dated D does hold day-D activity. Whichever it is, rows dated D-1 or earlier lie before the premiere, and that is the rule used here.
- **Markers inside the pre-release rows.** Rows before the premiere flagged `releasing`: 0. With a positive `averageScore`: 0. With an `episode` number: 1, in 1 titles. An episode number on D-1 means the first episode was on the schedule for the day before the listed premiere, which happens with late-night slots written as 24:00 or later. Where it occurs, D-1 is not safely pre-release, and a one-day embargo (cutoff at D-2) is the conservative choice.
- **Restatement cannot be ruled out from one snapshot.** Whether AniList ever rewrites old rows needs a second fetch on a later day and a row-by-row comparison.
- **Coverage depends on when the entry was created.** A title added to AniList after it aired has no pre-release rows at all, and obscure titles are the ones added late.

## 6. Can the ratings support a simulated item cold-start? (Task 5)

`rating_complete.csv` has 57,633,278 ratings from 310,059 users and no timestamps. Inside the Anime Compass catalog of 18,064 items that leaves 56,726,861 ratings and 30,875,410 positives (rating >= 8); 289,601 users have at least five. These reproduce the counts in Anime Compass's own split metadata. 15,269 catalog items have a positive at all and 2,514 have no rating of any kind, which are already cold in production and cannot be evaluated.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Catalog items | 15,269 | 11,582 | 8,693 | 7,393 | 6,428 | 3,253 |

| premiere 2016-2020, TV | 1,133 | 856 | 14,175 | 16.8% | 197,692 | 5,183,094 | 652 |
| random 20% of 2016-2020 TV (seed 590) | 227 | 175 | 15,047 | 3.2% | 170,776 | 975,321 | 779 |

- **How many warm items are needed.** This cannot be answered without training, and nothing was trained. What the counts show is that none of these cold sets comes close to starving the warm side: the largest hides 16.8% of positives and leaves 14,175 warm items. For scale, Anime Compass's evaluation ALS is trained on 24,916,911 of 30,875,410 positives, 19.3% fewer than production. Whether ALS quality on the warm side holds after the cut is an empirical check for the modelling stage.
- **Which items can be cold test items.** Only items with ratings, and in practice only those with enough positives to give a stable per-item metric. A second filter comes later: the item must also have pre-release rows on AniList.
- **It is a simulation.** With no timestamps, a user's warm history contains ratings given after the cold title aired. This measures whether features can place an unseen item among users whose tastes are otherwise fully known. It is not a pre-release backtest and should not be called one.

Keeping cold-item ratings out of everything:

1. Fix the cold item ids first, by premiere date or by seeded draw, and write them to a file with a checksum before touching ratings.
2. Split items three ways: warm (in ALS, and the only items a feature-to-factor mapping is fitted on), cold validation (model selection) and cold test (opened once). ALS never sees a validation or test item.
3. Build the ALS matrix from ratings filtered through that file, and assert in code that no cold id is in the matrix columns.
4. Compute every rating-derived feature (studio prior, franchise prior, popularity counts, co-occurrence) from the warm matrix only. MAL catalog columns such as `Members`, `Score`, `Favorites` and the `Score-N` counts are outcomes and stay out.
5. Use AniList fields graded `high` in section 2.5 for nothing, and `trends` only through the cutoff.
6. Train a fresh evaluation artifact under this directory. The production ALS and the existing evaluation ALS have both seen these items' ratings and are invalid here.
7. Draw evaluation users separately from the users Anime Compass has already scored, or state that they overlap.

## 7. Selection bias and limits of this stage

- **10 titles.** Every share above is a count out of 10; none supports a rate for the catalog.
- **The sample is conditioned on survival.** Titles were drawn from MAL's catalog as of early 2021 and looked up on AniList as it is today. A title's presence in both, and the day its AniList entry was created, are related to how much attention it got.
- **AniList's audience is its own.** English-language, tracker-using viewers. The outcome in the ratings is MAL users. Activity on one predicting ratings on the other is plausible but is cross-platform, not general demand.
- **Pre-2018 cohorts have `trending` only.** `popularity`, the closest thing to a plan-to-watch count, does not exist in the early rows.
- **Terms of use.** AniList prohibits hoarding and mass collection and says it is lenient for purely educational projects. A course pilot fits that; shipping collected AniList data inside the public Anime Compass app is a different use and would need their answer first.
